What a "partner" is

Mistral could sell every model licence itself, with its own salespeople calling every bank and carmaker in Europe. It cannot afford to, and customers do not want that anyway. Enterprises already buy from someone: their cloud provider, the consulting firm that runs their IT, the software vendor whose product they live in. A partner is any company that helps Mistral reach, sell to, deploy for, or serve a customer, and gets something for it.

ELI5

You bake the best bread in town. You can sell it from your own shop, but you reach far more people if supermarkets stock it (cloud providers), if caterers put it in their menus (systems integrators), if sandwich shops build on it (software vendors), and if a delivery service carries it (resellers). Each of those is a partner, each wants a cut, and each needs to know your rules.

What "operations" means here

Partner Managers (being hired in London, Paris and Munich right now) own relationships. A head of partner operations owns everything those people need to exist but should not be building themselves:

  • The number. A definition of partner-sourced, influenced and transacted revenue that Finance signs, and the partner plan the executive team commits to.
  • The economics. The partner P&L, the cost of partner-sourced revenue against direct, the rate card, the structures behind OEM, revenue-share, co-development and capacity deals.

When the direct team and a partner both claim a goal, you are the referee, and you wrote the rulebook before the match.

02Partner Operations 102

The mechanics you must be able to explain without notes.

Deal registration and rules of engagement

A partner tells Mistral, in a form, "we found this opportunity at HSBC for OCR on trade documents, roughly €800k." If approved, that partner is protected for a defined period: credit, margin or referral fee, and the direct sellers work with them rather than around them. Rules of engagement are the written policy for the edge cases. The two-edged part worth volunteering, from your own deal reviews: registration exists so the same opportunity cannot surface twice at two prices, but your own sellers can often reach a deeper discount than the partner is allowed, which leaves the partner exposed on a deal they sourced. Solex was the lived version: the same product was cheaper direct.

Sourced, influenced, transacted: the attribution fight

  • Sourced: the partner found the deal. Strictest, smallest, most trusted by Finance. At Confluent this was the number you judged the partner org on.
  • Influenced: the partner materially helped. Broad, easy to inflate, the number partner leaders love to quote.
  • Transacted (some companies say "fulfilled" or "through"): the partner is on the paper, whoever sourced it.

In a licence business attribution is paperwork. In a consumption business a partner deploys a workload in month one and the usage arrives across two years, mixed with the account's own organic growth. So partner credit has the same shape as rep credit: measured growth minus the account's cohort-expected trajectory. You built that on the direct side. It is an estimate, so it has to be paired with a cadence and a dispute forum, never sold as a receipt.

Marketplaces, private offers and CPPO

The hyperscalers run stores. Two things make them strategic: committed-spend burn-down (the customer's pre-approved cloud budget pays, so procurement takes days) and private offers (custom price and terms, transacted through the marketplace). AWS CPPO is the version with a consulting partner or reseller in the middle: you authorise the partner, the partner sets the customer price, the sale rides the customer's AWS bill, AWS takes a fee, the partner keeps the spread. You trade a few points for velocity. Mistral's partner-served deployment terms make the partner the customer's sole point of contact, so partner data feeds become the only truth about usage.

Programme design: tiers, incentives, funds

  • Tiers trade benefits for commitments: certified headcount, revenue targets, a joint business plan. Earn them on registered and closed commercial revenue, not logos or downloads. This matters doubly for open weights.
  • Margin and referral fees need a rate card and an approval matrix. Finance's job: benchmark what competitors and cohort companies pay, then test viability against your own margin. Calibrated, then constrained, not derived from first principles.
  • MDF, credits, funded POCs, SPIFs: plan-and-claim with proof, burn-down tracked, conversion reported. Fund programmes in tranches released as partner cohorts cross deployment gates; a programme funded in one lump is cut in one lump.

The operating cadence

Weekly pipeline and registration review, monthly reconciliation with Finance, quarterly business reviews per strategic partner, annual programme and tier review. Your Confluent stack maps directly: exec weeklies by initiative with eStaff in the room, monthly forecast review at eStaff, quarterly business reviews in the field and retros at eStaff. The cadence is the product: it is how a partner business becomes forecastable.

06The hardest problems, and what a good answer sounds like

What the interviewer is quietly worried about, answered with your own frameworks.

1. Attribution the CFO believes, in a consumption business

The company is headed for a $1B ARR claim and a possible listing. "Partner-influenced" cannot be a vanity number, and a partner influences usage it never transacts. Good answer: three numbers defined in writing and enforced in the CRM. Sourced requires registration before any Mistral opportunity existed and is deliberately conservative. Influenced requires a named, logged activity and is measured as the account's growth net of its cohort-expected trajectory, with quarterly true-ups and a standing dispute forum. Transacted is the paper. Say the honest part: it is an estimate that has to rank, not a receipt.

2. Deal structures that compound against Mistral

Cloudera, Ericsson and the SIs will want revenue shares; Microsoft has commitments; sovereign clouds want bespoke terms. Good answer: the compounding arithmetic (20% of a licence once versus 20% of a tripling stream forever), so partner economics go on influenced consumption or declining rates, not the whole stream. Then the three terms that decide an OEM deal: net share versus transfer price, bundle-discount allocation, and the floor. Then the reference structure: a template per deal type with a small number of negotiable variables, so the fifth sovereign-cloud agreement takes weeks.

3. Channel conflict with a forward-deployed direct team

Mistral's growth so far came from its own engineers embedded with customers. Good answer: in consumption, "who owns the customer" means who owns the usage telemetry and the expansion motion, per workload not per account, so conflict is a quarterly operating condition and needs a standing forum, not an escalation path. Direct sellers are paid neutrally on partner-transacted deals via retire-and-add rather than double-credit. And the two-edged registration failure mode, volunteered.

4. The hyperscalers are partners and competitors

Microsoft sells OpenAI, Google sells Gemini, AWS sells everything. Good answer: listing is not distribution. If the cloud rep is paid X for the house model you match it in some form (co-sell credit, SPIF, quota retirement) or your name never comes up. Then: be the easiest model vendor to transact (private-offer speed), and go through the clouds' sovereignty and regulated-industry teams rather than their general AI sellers. You lived this against MSK.

5. What partners get paid on, for an open-weight product

A partner can deploy the weights and never pay Mistral. Good answer: three ways to pay, three ecosystems. Pay on transacted revenue and you build resellers. Pay on influenced consumption and you build practice-builders. Pay on certified capacity and you build trainers. The right one is whichever you are short of, and Mistral is short of deployment capacity, so the design space is influenced consumption and certified capacity, with tiers earned on commercial outcomes and the money made on what partners cannot get free: Studio, Forge, Compute, support, indemnity. Guardrail: whatever you pay on must be measurable at the account level within the quarter you pay it.

6. Delivery capacity and the FDE seam

FDEs and partners both create consumption they do not transact. Good answer: who deploys is a cost and capacity question; who gets credit is an incentive question; keep them separate or you pay twice. FDE capacity is rival, so partners are the additive surge. Fund enablement per certified practitioner with a stated payback in influenced consumption, released in tranches as cohorts cross deployment gates. You re-resourced from AEs to SEs on median productivity evidence; this is the same argument.

7. Is the programme working, and how soon would you know

Good answer: compare cohort progression of partner-sourced accounts against direct-sourced accounts at the same consumption gates. If partner lands do not progress, the programme is manufacturing activity, and you want to know in quarter two. You ran exactly this on zero-commit lands at Confluent and it told you the belief was wrong. Then the fairness point: partner revenue lands later and noisier than direct; hold it to direct standards and you kill it before it works.

8. Speed and headcount

An 800-person company growing several-fold cannot wait for a 12-month build. Good answer: the definitions and the plan ship in a quarter on existing tools; say what is deliberately deferred; hire programme administration first and analytics second, each with a trigger.

Five ideas that are yours

These came out of the OpenAI prep and they are the difference between describing partner economics and having done them. Use each once, in a sentence.

  1. Three ways to pay, three ecosystems. Transacted revenue builds resellers; influenced consumption builds practice-builders; certified capacity builds trainers. Pay for the species you are short of.
  2. Partner margin on a stream compounds. A discount on a licence costs once; a share of a consumption stream costs on the customer's own growth forever. Hence bounties on influenced consumption and declining-rate structures. And the reframe: margin for capacity is a discount; margin for capability is an investment.
  3. Attribution net of organic, paired with a cadence. Credit is measured growth minus the account's cohort-expected line. It ranks, it does not receipt. The partner version and the rep version are the same maths.
  4. A partner's type tells you the only currency that moves it. Services firms want engagements; transacting firms want margin; embed partners want the transfer price; marketplaces want their sellers' comp. Paying the wrong currency is the most expensive channel mistake there is.
  5. Indirect and direct P&Ls must tie out. Split partner spend into deal-embedded economics (contra revenue, margin, fees; self-funding, governed by unit-economics floors) and capacity investment (partner managers, MDF, enablement, co-delivery hours, allocated field time). Every scarce resource has one home. If the decomposition does not close to total GTM cost, the allocation is built on sand.

Net of organic
Credit measured as an account's growth minus what accounts of its cohort do with no intervention.
Transfer price vs net share
Fixed price per unit to the partner (they fund discounts) versus a percentage of what they net (you absorb their discounting).
Floor / minimum royalty
The minimum the partner must pay or the threshold at which they engage; binds when volume disappoints.
Bundle-discount allocation
How a discount negotiated on a whole deal is spread across line items. Worth more than points of headline share.
Capacity commitment
Pre-agreed spend or compute. Moves cash, not revenue, until consumed.
Committed-spend burn-down
Marketplace purchases count against a customer's pre-agreed cloud spend, which is why they close faster.
Private offer / CPPO
A custom-priced deal through a marketplace; CPPO is AWS's version with a consulting partner or reseller in the middle.
Co-sell
Mistral and a partner's sellers working the same account with agreed roles and credit.
Rate card
Published margins, referral fees and rebates by partner type and tier.
MDF / SPIF
Market development funds claimed against proof; short-term cash incentives to partner sellers.
Cohort gate
A consumption threshold ($20K at Confluent) that separates a real workload from an experiment.
GSI / RSI / VAR / MSP
Global integrator (services), regional integrator (needs real margin), value-added reseller (margin), managed service provider (operates it).
OEM-in / OEM-out
You resell their technology (Oracle CDC) versus they resell yours (Solex). The economics invert.
Partner-served deployment
Mistral's term: the product delivered through a cloud provider or reseller who bills and supports the customer.
Forward-deployed
Mistral engineers embedded with a customer. The direct motion partners are meant to scale.
Open-weight
Model weights are published; anyone can run them. Mistral monetises what surrounds the weights.
Forge / Studio / Compute
Custom-model builder / application platform / European AI cloud.
